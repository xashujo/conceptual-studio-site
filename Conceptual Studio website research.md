# Win the college shortlist with a static site

Conceptual Studio's website will win work mainly by getting Josh onto a college budget holder's quote list and making him easy to set up as a supplier. Persuading strangers matters much less. Almost every job he is likely to win sits well below the Procurement Act's **£207,720** threshold, so it is bought under each institution's own rules, typically **two or three written quotes** from suppliers the buyer picks, and UK clients say agency websites rarely make clear what the agency is best at. The site should say in one plain sentence that he designs brand identities and data visualisation for colleges and public institutions. It should show Josh by name and face, with peer-college logos and named testimonials, tell case studies as problem, constraint and outcome, and publish per-service pricing ladders whose VAT-inclusive tiers sit under the common **£5k, £10k and £25k** quote bands. Procurement teams need one page of company, insurance and accessibility facts. On the technical side, a static Astro build on GitHub Pages handles everything on his list: native cross-document view transitions (about **89%** of browsers, with Firefox falling back to an ordinary page load), Pages CMS for editing projects, text, testimonials and the availability line without code, Web3Forms with Cloudflare Turnstile plus a Cal.com link for enquiries, an Astro-generated meta Content Security Policy, and cookieless analytics that remove the need for a cookie banner. Builder.io is the wrong tool: it has no Astro SDK, keeps content on its own servers and mostly solves layout editing he does not need. Two of his chosen components need defensive engineering. liquid-glass-js is a one-commit prototype that refracts a frozen screenshot of the page, so it should sit on top of a plain CSS glass layer rather than carry the navigation alone. The hero video's browser sniffing must send every iPhone and iPad browser the HEVC file, or be replaced with a single stacked-alpha video.

## Colleges buy below the tender line, through two or three written quotes

The Procurement Act 2023 has applied to colleges since February 2025 ([Tyne Coast College](https://www.tynecoast.ac.uk/assets/downloads/policies/financial-regulations-2026-2026-v29.pdf)), but from 1 January 2026 its full regime starts at **£207,720 including VAT** for sub-central bodies such as universities and academies ([LUPC](https://lupc.ac.uk/news-events/newsarticle/new-financial-thresholds-from-1-january-2026)). Below that line the duties thin out fast. A below-threshold contract only becomes "notifiable" at **£30,000** for non-central authorities, a buyer that invites specific suppliers need not advertise, and section 85 forbids a separate suitability or pre-qualification stage ([Cabinet Office guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69aeb1946827004e30b8a52a/2026-03-04_-_Guidance_-_Below_Threshold_FINAL_pdf_links_.docx.pdf); [LUPC](https://www.lupc.ac.uk/public-procurement/public-procurement/below-threshold-contracts/)). Section 86 asks buyers to consider the barriers SMEs face and lets below-threshold procedures favour UK-based suppliers and SMEs ([LUPC](https://www.lupc.ac.uk/public-procurement/public-procurement/below-threshold-contracts/)). What actually governs a £3k to £40k design job is each institution's financial regulations, and the published examples share a shape.

| Institution and policy | Lower band | Middle band | Upper band |
|---|---|---|---|
| Birmingham Metropolitan College, Dec 2025 ([BMET](https://www.bmet.ac.uk/app/uploads/2026/01/Finance-Regulations_Dec-2025_POL-395.pdf)) | Up to £10k: budget holder decides, "ideally three" quotes | £10k to £100k: three written quotes | Over £100k: three quotes approved by the CFO |
| Tyne Coast College, 2026 ([Tyne Coast](https://www.tynecoast.ac.uk/assets/downloads/policies/financial-regulations-2026-2026-v29.pdf)) | Up to £5k: value for money. £5,001 to £10k: two quotes, local supplier where possible | £10,001 to £24,999: three quotes. £25k to £50k: three quotes plus Contracts Finder | Over £50k: competitive tender |
| LETTA Trust, a multi-academy trust, 2025-28 ([LETTA](https://hermitage.towerhamlets.sch.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/The-LETTA-Trust-Tendering-Policy-2025-28-FINAL.pdf)) | Under £10k: low value | £10k to £40k: at least three written quotes | Over £40k: high value, two people score bids |

All three count VAT. BMET and Tyne Coast value contracts over their whole life, and BMET bans order splitting. In practice a college marketing head can email three studios without publishing anything, which gives the website two jobs: make Josh the obvious name to include, and make him painless to set up.

The person sending those emails is usually the head or director of marketing. Esher College's head of marketing reports to the Principal and is asked "to tender and work with external agencies as appropriate" and to keep "consistency in brand messaging and visual identity" ([Esher College](https://esher.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Head-of-Marketing-JD.pdf)). Sign-off climbs with value. At BMET a budget holder approves up to £50k and vice principals up to £100k, while Tyne Coast adds the COO from £10k and the CEO from £50k ([BMET](https://www.bmet.ac.uk/app/uploads/2026/01/Finance-Regulations_Dec-2025_POL-395.pdf); [Tyne Coast](https://www.tynecoast.ac.uk/assets/downloads/policies/financial-regulations-2026-2026-v29.pdf)). So the marketing head usually has to sell Josh internally. In the 2025 What Clients Think research (680 UK client interviews), **52%** said convincing internal stakeholders is a challenge and **82%** said the winning agency is usually chosen unanimously ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/ai-costs-and-confusing-websites-heres-what-clients-think/)). Anything on the site should survive being forwarded to a finance director.

That finance director is also a buyer in their own right. College accounts for 2025-26 go to DfE by 31 December 2026 and must be on the college website by **31 January 2027**, with a financial review "written in terms which a non-accountant would understand", and colleges are encouraged to publish a voluntary carbon disclosure alongside ([College accounts direction](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69c280efb920af63be1c7752/college-accounts-direction-2025-to-2026.pdf)). Accountability statements had a revised deadline of **31 July 2026** ([DfE](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dfe-update-14-january-2026/dfe-update-further-education-14-january-2026)), and DfE expected every college, university and trust to have a sustainability lead and a climate action plan by 2025 ([DfE guidance](https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/id/eprint/39845/1/Sustainability%20leadership%20and%20climate%20action%20plans%20in%20education%20-%20GOV.UK.pdf)). These dates create fixed, recurring demand for the data visualisation Josh sells, and give him a calendar for outreach and writing.

Shortlists also form before anyone gets in touch. Hinge's 2014 survey of 1,028 buyers found that over 99% check a firm out and over 80% visit its website ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/new-research-study-how-professional-services-buyers-check-you-out)). 6sense's 2025 survey of over 4,000 buyers, mostly of technology, found that **94%** of buying groups had ranked vendors before first contact and 77% bought from that early favourite ([6sense via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en)). Both firms sell marketing, but the direction is consistent: visitors mostly arrive through a referral, a LinkedIn post or a colleague, and the site's job is to confirm that instinct.

Larger institutional brand work sits inside multi-year frameworks, which are a later target. Nottingham Trent's creative services framework drew 17 tenders per lot, all from SMEs ([Find a Tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/018326-2025)), and Huddersfield's final-stage bidders were all SMEs, with a separate £160k sub-brand lot won by small studios ([Find a Tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/077819-2025); [Find a Tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/084426-2025)). Glasgow's graphic design framework, which explicitly covered infographics, asked for £5m public and employer's liability cover and turnover of about one times the contract value ([Find a Tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/028515-2025)), tests that can shut a one-person company out of large lots. The CPC print and design lot that DfE recommends is print-led, covering "basic design and page layout" with mainly print firms as suppliers ([CPC](https://www.thecpc.ac.uk/services/frameworks/print-and-design-services)), so identity and data design largely sit outside it. The Crown Commercial Service's Communications Marketplace requires Cyber Essentials and a DUNS number ([Find a Tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/020398-2026)). Direct quotes come first.

## One plain sentence should name the sector and both services

UK buyers say clearly what they want from an agency homepage. In What Clients Think 2025, **61%** said it is hard to tell what an agency is best at from its website, **70%** wanted a homepage statement that sums the agency up, **79%** wanted agencies to say more clearly which clients they want, and **67%** said small agencies should focus on their strengths rather than go full-service ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/ai-costs-and-confusing-websites-heres-what-clients-think/); [DBA](https://www.dba.org.uk/5-take-aways-from-the-2025-what-clients-think-report/)). Hinge calls industry focus "the only specialization approach that comes with a built-in marketing advantage" and says firms combining two areas of focus end up with "fewer (and often no)" direct competitors ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/to-specialize-or-not-to-specialize)). Hinge sells positioning advice and publishes few figures openly, so read that as directional. Jonathan Stark adds the practical point: a skills-only position "forces your potential buyers to magically recognize that your specialized skill is the solution", while a market position lets them recognise themselves ([Jonathan Stark](https://jonathanstark.com/daily/20170916-can-i-position-myself-by-target-skill-instead-of-target-market)).

Josh's institutions-first choice with two headline services is exactly that combination: a sector buyers recognise, joined to a discipline pairing few studios offer. The premium end of the market treats identity plus data as a real offer. Clever°Franke lists "data-driven brands" and "data design systems" among its services ([Clever°Franke](https://www.cleverfranke.com/)), and Pentagram describes Giorgia Lupi's work as combining data and storytelling into "unique and singular brand expressions" ([Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/about/giorgia-lupi)). The research found no UK data-design studio specialising in further or higher education, which suggests the niche is open, though it does not prove it. The current line, "Visual & Graphic Designer... helping brands, artists and creators", names neither a sector nor the pairing, and describes buyers he mostly does not serve.

A workable hero reads "Brand identity and data visualisation for colleges and public institutions", followed by one line of proof such as "Identities, annual reports and data maps for UK colleges, designed in London", with "Start a project" as the main button and "See the work" beside it. A short "Who I work with" line (colleges, universities, multi-academy trusts, charities and public bodies) answers the 79% directly. These lines are illustrations; the research supports the pattern rather than the exact words.

Keeping 3D and video as supporting work has the same backing. **68%** of clients are unimpressed by showreels, while 63% like video inside case studies ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/ai-costs-and-confusing-websites-heres-what-clients-think/)), so the showreel should not open the homepage. Video earns its place attached to the two core offers: a motion version of an identity, a film cut of an annual report, or an animated data story inside a case study. Commercial and international clients such as Fou Answer in Tokyo can sit in a "Selected commercial work" group that shows range without blurring the position.

The copy should be plain, short and factual, leaving the classical gold visuals to carry the premium signal. Studio Dumbar states its offer in one sentence and lists client names ([Studio Dumbar](https://studiodumbar.com/)), DixonBaxi gives each project a single verb-led headline ([DixonBaxi](https://dixonbaxi.com/)), and Koto proves scale with numbers, such as an "18-month project across 15 markets and more than 50 sub-brands" ([Koto](https://koto.com/)). The Fully Saturated review of 150 UK design websites found the same phrases everywhere, from "stand out in a crowded market" to "we bring brands to life", and quoted one client: "Clients don't understand the bollocks we talk" ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/buzzword-bingo-do-branding-agencies-all-sound-the-same/)). Institutional readers also write under GOV.UK's plain-English rules, which tell writers to avoid "deliver", "empower", "facilitate", "impact", "key", "leverage", "robust", "streamline" and "transform" ([GOV.UK style guide](https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/style-guides/a-to-z-style-guide/)). Copy that follows those rules reads as fluent to a college marketing team. A project headline in that register would be something like "Mapping campus carbon data for New City College's sustainability report".

"Conceptual Studio" as the name, "I" in the copy and Josh pictured is the strongest combination. Andy Clarke puts it bluntly: if you mainly work alone, say "I", because "the moment a prospective client asks, 'How many people are you?' you'll have some uncomfortable explaining to do" ([24 ways](https://datasette-sqlite-fts4.datasette.io/24ways-fts4/articles/51)). Elizabeth Goodspeed notes that Lotta Nieminen avoids "the all-powerful 'we'" while still calling her practice a studio, and that some clients seek individuals because they are tired of agency overhead ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/elizabeth-goodspeed-how-many-designers-does-it-take-to-make-a-studio-creative-industry-041225)). **78%** of clients are frustrated when an agency's principals are not shown ([DBA](https://www.dba.org.uk/5-take-aways-from-the-2025-what-clients-think-report/)), and public tenders test capacity anyway: North Ayrshire asked for the names of staff assigned and disclosure of subcontracting above 25% ([Find a Tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/056185-2025)). Josh can answer the capacity question before it comes up with a line naming the roles in his network (print, large-format print, development), describing them by role rather than by company wherever the arrangement is white-label. The single point of contact then becomes a selling point: "You work with me directly, from first meeting to final file." No study compares "I" and "we" with institutional buyers, so this rests on practitioner judgement.

## Proof, people and paperwork decide who gets the quote request

David C. Baker's rule for a lean studio homepage is to declare the positioning, give contact details and offer a way to receive your thinking ([Newfangled](https://www.newfangled.com/?p=10834)). Put that together with the buyer evidence and the homepage order falls out: hero and button, a "Trusted by" strip of client logos, three to six case studies with education first, the two services linking to their pricing ladders, one named testimonial, a short "How I work" sequence, Josh's introduction with photo, price guidance and availability, then a full enquiry block and a footer with company details. Fewer, deeper case studies suit buyers who are already swamped: 56% of B2B buyers say they are overwhelmed by content ([Demand Gen Report](https://demandgenreport.com/blog/the-top-5-content-recommendations-for-marketers-according-to-b2b-buyers/47187)). The rest of the site follows the same logic: Work (filterable by sector), a Services page each for identity and data visualisation, About, a "Working with public sector clients" page, Notes, Contact, a privacy notice and an accessibility statement. No study measures hero length, number of featured projects or sticky buttons on studio sites, so this order is reasoned rather than tested.

Evidence on logo strips for service firms does not exist in the sources found; NN/g's B2B trust research does not discuss them ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-trust-from-b2c/)). The case for a strip rests on peer recognition. A London college marketing head who sees New City College, Southbank College Group and ACL Essex learns in a second that Josh knows their world, and grouping logos by sector (education, commercial, international) keeps that cue sharp. Get written permission first, since many colleges have brand-use rules. Testimonials have better support. NN/g's eyetracking found readers look at the author's **job title and organisation**, and were most persuaded by testimonials that start with a reservation and end in confidence ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-trust-from-b2c/)). Each quote should carry a full name, role and institution, ideally with a "we weren't sure about X, but" arc, and appear on the homepage, at the end of the relevant case study and beside the enquiry form, rather than on a separate testimonials page few buyers visit.

Case studies need a different structure from the usual gallery. **66%** of clients say case studies do not tell them what they need, especially the problem solved, and 65% want more before-and-after examples ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/ai-costs-and-confusing-websites-heres-what-clients-think/)). Blair Enns's argument that diagnosis and strategy come before creative execution ([Win Without Pitching review](https://mickmel.substack.com/p/the-win-without-pitching-manifesto)) points towards showing the thinking as well as the output. A template that serves both: an at-a-glance panel (client, sector, year, scope, Josh's role), the challenge in the client's words, the diagnosis and approach, the outcome in use, an inline testimonial, a related next project, and a closing prompt such as "Planning something similar for your college?". Data-visualisation studies should show the raw spreadsheet, the finished graphic, how accessibility was handled and where the work ran. That is how Accurat's report clients describe value: expertise, deadlines and making information "accessible to citizens" ([Accurat](https://accurat.it/reports)). Concrete outcomes beat adjectives, for example a college identity trademarked and run across London buses and the Underground, or a sustainability report and searchable map published on time. Each study can serve two readers at once: the marketing director, who cares about roll-out and sign-off, and the finance director, who cares about deadlines and accuracy.

Accessibility is Josh's sharpest differentiator, because it is the buyer's legal problem. Public sector websites must meet **WCAG 2.2 AA** and publish an accessibility statement ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/accessibility-requirements-for-public-sector-websites-and-apps)). DfE's manual puts PDFs and Word files published after 23 September 2018 in scope ([DfE Accessibility Manual](https://accessibility.education.gov.uk/standards/accessibility-regulations)), and the Government Analysis Function says the standard "includes charts", which need a text alternative or data table, must not rely on colour alone and should come with an accessible data download ([Analysis Function](https://analysisfunction.civilservice.gov.uk/policy-store/data-visualisation-charts/)). The GOV.UK page does not name FE colleges explicitly, so the claim is best phrased around what he makes ("accessible annual reports, tagged PDFs, charts with data tables") rather than as a legal statement about every client. His own site should meet WCAG 2.2 AA and carry its own accessibility statement, which turns the site itself into evidence.

People and place matter too. NN/g's B2B studies found that visible people and a physical location help buyers judge a firm legitimate, and recommend offering at least an email address and a phone number ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-trust-from-b2c/)). A photo, a short first-person bio (Kingston School of Art, then design lead at a Tokyo startup) and a line such as "Based in London, working with clients across the UK, Europe and Japan" cover this, with Conceptual Studio Limited's company number and registered office in the footer. A promise to reply within one working day costs nothing and plays to a solo studio's strength; research from 2011 concluded that "most companies are not responding nearly fast enough" to online leads ([HBR](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)), though its headline figures sit behind a paywall. A small Notes section also earns its keep. In the 2024 Edelman-LinkedIn survey of 3,484 executives, UK respondents included, **73%** said thought leadership is a more trustworthy basis for judging capability than marketing materials, and **86%** were likely to invite a consistent producer of it into an RFP ([Edelman-LinkedIn](https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2024-02/_2024%20Edelman-LinkedIn%20B2B%20Thought%20Leadership%20Impact%20Report%20Final.pdf)). LinkedIn was the only social platform whose use by professional-services buyers rose ([Hinge](https://hingemarketing.com/blog/story/four-professional-services-findings-from-our-inside-the-buyers-brain-research)). Short pieces timed to the sector calendar, such as making annual accounts readable for non-accountants before January, should stay ungated, since nearly two-thirds of buyers will not fill in a form to read gated content ([Demand Gen Report](https://demandgenreport.com/blog/the-top-5-content-recommendations-for-marketers-according-to-b2b-buyers/47187)).

The procurement page handles the second job, getting set up as a supplier. The research found no published FE supplier-onboarding form, so the list below combines what design tenders asked for with what the law requires.

## Price each ladder tier to land under a quote band

Josh's planned per-service pricing ladders are well supported. NN/g found that "showing prices builds trust through transparency", that hidden prices look like concealment and risk sticker shock, and that firms with variable pricing should show a starting price or a range for common scenarios ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-trust-from-b2c/); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-prices-for-common-scenarios/)). Accurat already sells data design this way: a print report for $5,000 in four weeks, an interactive report for $7,500, both together for $10,000, and a premium tier priced on request ([Accurat Reports](https://accurat.it/reports)).

The procurement rules add a twist worth designing for. Quote bands include VAT and count the whole contract, so a tier priced at £9,500 plus VAT costs the college £11,400. That moves a BMET purchase from budget-holder discretion into three written quotes, and a Tyne Coast purchase from two quotes to three ([BMET](https://www.bmet.ac.uk/app/uploads/2026/01/Finance-Regulations_Dec-2025_POL-395.pdf); [Tyne Coast](https://www.tynecoast.ac.uk/assets/downloads/policies/financial-regulations-2026-2026-v29.pdf)). If Conceptual Studio is VAT-registered, ceilings of about **£4,150, £8,300 and £20,800 plus VAT** keep tiers under £5k, £10k and £25k including VAT. The aim is to make a purchase easy to approve, not to get round the rules: BMET forbids order splitting and values multi-year contracts over their full term, so tiers must be genuinely different scopes and a retainer counts at its total value. The structure below is illustrative; the research supports the shape, and the prices are Josh's to set.

| Tier | Brand identity | Data visualisation | Target ceiling, VAT included |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | Identity refresh or sub-brand for one department or campaign | One infographic, chart set or heat map | Under £5k |
| Core | Full identity system with guidelines | Report data design for annual accounts or a sustainability report | Under £10k |
| Extended | Identity system plus roll-out: prospectus, signage, campaign templates, motion version | Interactive or searchable map, data microsite, or a report with a web version | Under £25k |
| Custom | Group or multi-campus brand | Multi-report programme or dashboard | Custom quote |

Each tier should show a "from" price, a typical lead time and what is included, with an "Ask about this" button that opens the quote form with that tier already selected. Motion and 3D appear as add-ons inside tiers, which keeps them in a supporting role.

The custom-quote form should be short. The only field-count data found is an aggregator compilation without clear primary sources, so treat it as directional: median conversion fell from 23.1% at three fields to 17.0% at five and 11.4% at seven, and splitting a form into steps only helped from about six fields and was slightly worse at three ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/form-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-data-points)). Budget and timeline fields reportedly cost some volume but are the fields sales teams value most ([Brixon Group](https://brixongroup.com/en/lead-forms-in-b2b-the-perfect-balancing-act-between-data-depth-and-conversion-rate)), again on unverified figures. For a one-person studio protecting its time, a sensible single-step form has four required fields (name, work email, organisation, and what you need, pre-filled from the tier clicked) and two optional ones. The first is a budget band matching the procurement bands: under £5k, £5k to £10k, £10k to £25k, £25k to £50k, over £50k, or not sure yet. The second is a "needed by" date, which matters to buyers planning around enrolment and publication deadlines. An optional tickbox reading "I need a formal written quote for procurement" signals fluency at no cost. NN/g recommends explaining why you ask for information ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-trust-from-b2c/)), so the budget field should carry helper text such as "A rough band helps me suggest the right scope." Neither Web3Forms nor Formspree allows file uploads on its free plan ([Web3Forms pricing](https://web3forms.com/pricing); [Formspree](https://formspree.io/plans)), so a visible email address with "or email your brief" covers the institutional habit of sending a PDF. Proper `autocomplete` attributes on name, email and organisation help mobile autofill.

No independent study compares a calendar booking button with a form for studios. The one data point, a vendor case study from a software firm with no control group, saw booked meetings rise from 40% to 90% of requests once a calendar appeared straight after the form ([Chili Piper](https://www.chilipiper.com/customer-story/saasoptics)), which supports form first and calendar second. Cal.com's free plan has unlimited event types and calendars, with branding removal needing its Teams plan at $12 per user per month ([Cal.com](https://cal.com/pricing)). Calendly's free plan allows one event type and one calendar ([Calendly](https://calendly.com/pricing)). "Book a 20-minute call" works as a secondary link and on the thank-you screen, pointing to the Cal.com page rather than an embed, so no third-party script loads on every page.

The availability line works as planning information rather than pressure. No study shows that availability pills or "two slots left" lines lift B2B enquiries. The scarcity research is consumer-focused, such as a 2026 hotel-booking experiment ([White Rose](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/236107)), and "Booking Q1 2026, 2 slots left" already appears in generic AI-built templates ([example template](https://ceph-creations.lovable.app/projects/studio)). B2B buyers are cautious: 70% told 6sense that economic anxiety shaped their vendor choice, often towards incumbents ([6sense via BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en)). For a college working to an academic year, "Booking new projects from February 2027" answers a real question, whereas the current "Available to book a call" pill says nothing, since calls are always available. A stale date undermines trust, so availability should be stored in the CMS as a month plus a short note rather than free text, and the page should hide the line once that month has passed (a weekly scheduled rebuild does this without extra JavaScript). A static dot suits the tone better than a pulsing one.

Mobile shapes first impressions more than finished enquiries. Siege Media's analysis of 13.4 billion sessions puts a typical B2B site at **28% mobile** in the first half of 2026, against 57% for consumer sites ([Siege Media](https://siegemedia.com/research/mobile-vs-desktop-traffic-share)), and aggregator data shows five-field B2B forms converting at 12.8% on desktop against 8.7% on mobile ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/form-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026-data-points)). People will find Josh on their phones through LinkedIn and social video and then enquire from a desk, so the mobile homepage needs the positioning line, logos and a thumb-reachable "Start a project" bar within its first two screens.

## A static Astro build covers the list, apart from response headers

Astro, at version 7.4 since 15 October 2026, remains the right framework ([Astro blog](https://astro.build/blog/)). It outputs real static HTML for each page, runs React Bits components as islands only where needed, and turns images in `src/` into AVIF and WebP at build time while copying anything in `public/` untouched ([Astro images](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/images/)). The official GitHub Pages workflow uses `withastro/action` and `actions/deploy-pages`, with Settings > Pages > Source set to GitHub Actions; on a custom domain, `site` must be set to the domain and `base` removed, or links break ([Astro deploy guide](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/deploy/github/)). Next.js static export, by contrast, loses default image optimisation, redirects and headers ([Next.js](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/static-exports)). For the domain, add it in GitHub before changing DNS, point the apex at GitHub's four A records and `www` at a CNAME, verify the domain so nobody else can claim it, and avoid wildcard records ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/configuring-a-custom-domain-for-your-github-pages-site/managing-a-custom-domain-for-your-github-pages-site)). Pages allows a 1 GB site and 100 GB of monthly bandwidth as soft limits, exempts custom Actions workflows from the ten-builds-an-hour cap, and says it "isn't meant as free hosting for commercial sites, such as online businesses, e-commerce stores, or sites offering commercial software as a service" ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits)). A portfolio with an enquiry form is very likely fine; Cloudflare Pages or Netlify are the fallback if GitHub ever disagrees.

The animation libraries carry licence terms worth knowing in a public repo. GSAP and all its formerly paid plugins, SplitText and ScrollTrigger included, are now free for commercial use ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free); [GSAP docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Installation/)), but under Webflow's proprietary licence, which Webflow can change or end, so the version should be pinned ([ScanCode LicenseDB](https://scancode-licensedb.aboutcode.org/gsap-standard-no-charge-2025.html)). React Bits is MIT plus Commons Clause, which allows use as part of a website but not resale of the components ([React Bits licence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/DavidHDev/react-bits/main/LICENSE.md)). Thoughtworks flagged Lenis smooth scrolling for accessibility problems "which could confuse users with disabilities" ([Thoughtworks](https://www.thoughtworks.com/radar/languages-and-frameworks/lenis)); that assessment dates from 2024, but native scrolling with ScrollTrigger remains the safer default.

Page transitions can be native CSS, with no router. Cross-document view transitions (`@view-transition { navigation: auto; }`) work in Chrome and Edge 126+ and Safari 18.2+ on Mac and iOS, about **88.8%** of global usage ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/cross-document-view-transitions)). Firefox is the gap. caniuse calls its support "partial" from version 144, MDN's compatibility data lists it as unsupported ([MDN BCD](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/main/css/at-rules/view-transition.json)), and Firefox 144's release notes mention only same-document transitions ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Mozilla/Firefox/Releases/144)), so assume Firefox users get an instant page load. Astro's `<ClientRouter />` would animate everywhere and switches animation off under reduced motion, but it forces every script to re-initialise after each navigation and is not supported by Astro's built-in CSP ([Astro view transitions](https://docs.astro.build/en/guides/view-transitions/); [Astro configuration](https://docs.astro.build/en/reference/configuration-reference/)). The better pattern is native transitions wrapped in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, with a shared `view-transition-name` on a case-study thumbnail and its hero image so one morphs into the other. ClientRouter is only worth it if something must keep running across pages. One side effect to plan for: on a multi-page site every navigation reloads the page, which re-runs any liquid-glass screenshot, covered below.

Security on GitHub Pages is good for HTTPS and limited beyond it. Ticking "Enforce HTTPS" gets a Let's Encrypt certificate for the custom domain ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/securing-your-github-pages-site-with-https)), but Pages cannot set response headers and, according to a long-running community thread, does not send HSTS on custom domains by default, though GitHub Support reportedly adds it on request ([GitHub Community](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/4444)). Astro's `security.csp` option writes a meta Content Security Policy with script and style hashes into every page and has to be tested on a build rather than the dev server ([Astro configuration](https://docs.astro.build/en/reference/configuration-reference/)). Header-only protections such as HSTS and clickjacking defence need a proxy. Cloudflare's free plan can add HSTS and set headers through Transform Rules ([Cloudflare HSTS](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/edge-certificates/additional-options/http-strict-transport-security/); [Cloudflare Transform Rules](https://developers.cloudflare.com/rules/transform/response-header-modification/)), but renewal of GitHub's certificate behind Cloudflare's proxy can fail, and one participant reports that GitHub's unofficial advice is not to proxy at all ([Let's Encrypt Community](https://community.letsencrypt.org/t/fyi-for-github-pages-or-vercel-netlify-with-cloudflare/231054)). For a brochure site with no logins, plain GitHub Pages with enforced HTTPS and Astro's meta CSP is the sensible choice, and the remaining risk is small. If headers ever become essential, the same build can move to a host that reads a headers file.

Cookie compliance becomes almost trivial. The site has no login, so it sets no session cookies and the `Secure`, `HttpOnly` and `SameSite` flags have nothing to apply to. Choosing a few deliberate black sections over a full dark theme also removes the only first-party storage the site would otherwise have had, a remembered theme preference. That leaves analytics. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 added a PECR exception for storage used solely to collect statistics that improve a website ([Blake Morgan](https://www.blakemorgan.co.uk/data-use-and-access-act-2025-cookies)), and the ICO says it covers aggregate statistics if users get clear information and a "simple and free" way to object, and if any third-party provider acts only as your processor ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guidance-on-the-use-of-storage-and-access-technologies/what-are-the-exceptions/)). Cloudflare Web Analytics is free, does not collect visitors' personal data and needs no DNS change ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/web-analytics/about/)), and GoatCounter stores no IP addresses or user agents ([GoatCounter](https://www.goatcounter.com/help/gdpr)). Either should mean no cookie banner, though a short privacy notice with an opt-out is still needed. Google Analytics 4 is best avoided, because advertising-linked use falls outside the exception. Third-party embeds such as Cal.com, YouTube or Vimeo should load only when clicked.

Web3Forms is the better free form service here: 250 submissions a month, an access key designed to be public, and support for Cloudflare Turnstile ([Web3Forms FAQ](https://docs.web3forms.com/getting-started/faq); [Web3Forms pricing](https://web3forms.com/pricing)). Formspree's free plan gives only 50 a month, though it includes domain restriction, which Web3Forms keeps for its $12-a-month Pro plan ([Formspree](https://formspree.io/plans)). Turnstile is free, works without other Cloudflare services and claims WCAG 2.2 AAA compliance ([Cloudflare Turnstile](https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/plans/)); it should load on the contact page only, alongside a hidden honeypot field. Both form services are US companies with no stated UK or EU data residency, and Web3Forms contradicts itself on retention (its FAQ says it does not store submissions, while its pricing page lists a 30-day history and retention of up to three years). The privacy notice should name it as a processor, and Josh should read its data processing terms before launch.

The black sections are simple with CSS `light-dark()`, Baseline since May 2024. Setting `color-scheme: dark` on an element forces the dark value for everything inside it ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/color_value/light-dark)), so tokens defined once, such as ink as `light-dark(#000, #F1DFC8)` and paper as `light-dark(#F1DFC8, #000)`, flip automatically inside any section marked dark, buttons, form controls and the glass navigation included. With no toggle there is no flash-prevention script to write and nothing to store.

On GitHub Free, Pages only works from a public repository ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/pages/getting-started-with-github-pages/github-pages-limits)), so everything committed, history included, is public. The site needs no secrets: the Web3Forms key and Turnstile site key are public by design, and Turnstile's secret stays with the form service. Push protection is on by default for personal accounts and blocks recognised secret formats ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/code-security/secret-scanning/introduction/about-push-protection)). The bigger risk is content: unreleased client work, NDA material and source files belong elsewhere. If a secret ever leaks, rotate it first, then rewrite history with `git-filter-repo` 2.47 or later and ask GitHub Support to clear cached views ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/authentication/keeping-your-account-and-data-secure/removing-sensitive-data-from-a-repository)). One check the research did not cover: whether the Agatho licence allows the web font files to be served from a public repository, where anyone can download them directly.

## Liquid glass and the alpha hero need fallbacks before flourishes

Both signature effects add weight to the page's first moments, and speed is the best-evidenced driver of conversion. Portent found B2B lead-generation sites loading in one second converted about **three times** as well as those loading in five ([Portent](https://www.portent.com/blog/analytics/research-site-speed-hurting-everyone.htm)). Its data is from 2022 and its definition of load time is loose, but the ratio is the useful part. The text, button and a still poster should render first, with glass and video layered on afterwards.

### liquid-glass-js refracts a frozen screenshot

liquid-glass-js has exactly one commit, from 12 June 2025, no npm package and no React wrapper ([GitHub](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js)). It depends on html2canvas 1.4.1, which has had no release since January 2022, while a maintained fork, html2canvas-pro, reached 2.5.2 in October 2026 ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/html2canvas); [npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/html2canvas-pro)). The source shows how it works. The first glass element takes **one html2canvas screenshot of the whole page body**, shared by every later element, and the library never takes another; only the demo re-captures, on resize ([container.js](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js/blob/main/container.js); [demo.js](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js/blob/main/demo.js)). On scroll the shader offsets into that screenshot by `scrollY`, so a fixed navigation bar does refract the right region of the page beneath it. But it refracts a still image. html2canvas copies a playing video as one frozen frame, or as a blank canvas if the video is cross-origin ([html2canvas source](https://registry.npmjs.org/html2canvas/-/html2canvas-1.4.1.tgz)); content hidden for a scroll-reveal animation is captured invisible; lazy images and late-loading fonts may be missing; hover states never appear.

The costs are real. An open issue from November 2025 reports noticeable scroll lag when the glass sits in a fixed navbar, with no reply from the maintainer ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js/issues/2)). Every pixel runs a 13 by 13 blur plus a 660-read gradient loop, and the canvas ignores device pixel ratio ([container.js](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js/blob/main/container.js)), so a 390 by 80 pixel mobile bar needs roughly 24 million texture reads per frame at 1x, and its edges look soft on Retina screens. The README advertises WebGL 2, but the code asks for WebGL 1 ([README](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js/blob/main/README.md)). The components are plain `div`s with no role, tab stop or ARIA attributes, and button text defaults to white ([button.js](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js/blob/main/button.js); [glass.css](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js/blob/main/glass.css)). Two failures are silent. The shader drops alpha, so any transparent area in the screenshot renders black unless the body has an opaque background, and a single cross-origin image without CORS headers taints the canvas, which under standard canvas rules stops all the glass from rendering.

The deliberate black sections add a further requirement. The screenshot contains them, so the geometry stays right, but the library hard-codes a white-to-grey tint ([container.js](https://github.com/dashersw/liquid-glass-js/blob/main/container.js)), and a cream bar with black text will fail over black content, so the tint has to switch as the bar crosses a dark section. Contrast arithmetic sets the levels. Black on cream is 16.1:1. Over a worst-case black backdrop, a cream tint needs at least **55% opacity for 4.5:1** and 70% for 7:1, and cream text on a near-black tint needs at least 65% opacity over a white backdrop. The library's default 20% white tint falls far short of either.

Other web techniques trade live content against true refraction.

| Technique | Shows live content | Bends the image | Chrome and Edge | Safari, Mac and iOS | Firefox |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `backdrop-filter: blur() saturate()` with tint and rim | Yes | No | Yes | Yes, 18+ unprefixed | Yes, 103+ |
| `backdrop-filter: url()` with an SVG displacement map | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| WebGL over an html2canvas screenshot (liquid-glass-js) | No, static | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

Plain `backdrop-filter` is supported across all three engines ([MDN BCD](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/backdrop-filter.json)). The SVG displacement route, documented by kube.io, works only where browsers accept SVG filters as a backdrop filter, which is Chromium alone ([kube.io](https://kube.io/blog/liquid-glass-css-svg/)). WebKit's bug for it is still open; as of its last update on 5 September 2026, patches under review were crashing the GPU process on trunk, so no Safari date exists ([WebKit Bugzilla](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=245510)). The popular liquid-glass-react package confirms that in Safari and Firefox "displacement will not be visible" ([GitHub](https://github.com/rdev/liquid-glass-react)).

The safer build is layered. Every visitor gets a semantic `<nav>` with real links, styled as cream glass at 60 to 70% opacity with `backdrop-filter: blur(16px) saturate(140%)`, an inset highlight and a hairline rim, switching inside black sections to a warm near-black tint with a faint cream rim. Chromium browsers can add live displacement refraction on top. liquid-glass-js then becomes optional. It can be used for one contained element over a static region, or as a hardened fork for the bar: capture after fonts and images load, force animation end states in the captured copy, keep media same-origin, set an opaque body background, slim the shader, cap pixel ratio at about 2, re-capture only when the width changes, render only while visible, and use one container holding real links rather than nested buttons. Because a multi-page site reloads on every navigation, even the hardened version pays the screenshot cost on every page view.

Accessibility settings make the plain layer non-negotiable. `prefers-reduced-transparency` works only in Chromium 118 and later, not in Safari or on iOS ([MDN BCD](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/at-rules/media.json)), so an iPhone user who has turned on Reduce Transparency, or picked iOS 26.1's Tinted glass ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/phones/iphones/ios-26-1-lets-you-adjust-liquid-glass-transparency-on-your-iphone-heres-how-to-do-it)), cannot be detected, and the default glass must be legible on its own. `prefers-contrast` works everywhere ([MDN BCD](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/at-rules/media.json)) and should switch to near-opaque glass with a solid border. NN/g's October 2025 review of iOS 26 found translucent controls "often invisible" and the collapsing tab bar "less predictable" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)), and Apple's own guidance says not to use Liquid Glass in the content layer and to use it sparingly ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials.md)). For Josh that means glass on navigation only, never over the hero wordmark, a mobile tab bar that stays put once it appears, 44-pixel tap targets, an active state that does not rely on colour, and no wobble under reduced motion. With mobile at around 28% of B2B sessions, the tab bar shapes first impressions more than enquiries, which argues for keeping the effort spent on it proportionate.

### Every iPhone and iPad browser needs the HEVC file

Safari still cannot play VP9 video with alpha, so HEVC with alpha remains its only native route ([Rotato](https://rotato.app/read/transparent-videos-for-the-web)), and Chrome "cannot decode `hvc1` in most configurations" ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/transparent-video-web-hevc-alpha-ffmpeg)). Josh's current rule, HEVC for Safari and Instagram and WebM for everyone else, misses a structural fact: Apple requires every iOS app that browses the web to use WebKit ([Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), and the EU alternative-engine entitlement applies only to apps whose main purpose is browsing ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/alternative-browser-engines/)). Chrome and Firefox on iOS, and the in-app browsers in Facebook, TikTok, LinkedIn and Gmail, are all WebKit underneath, and today they get a WebM file they cannot show with transparency. The gate should be the engine. Send HEVC to any iPhone or iPad (including iPads that report a Mac user agent but have a touchscreen) and to macOS browsers whose user agent contains AppleWebKit without Chrome, Edge or Firefox tokens; send VP9 WebM to everything else, including all Android in-app browsers. The shortcuts fail. Listing the HEVC source first, as Rotato and hontran.dev suggest, would hand HEVC to Chrome on macOS, which Josh has seen play it without alpha. `canPlayType` cannot tell alpha from non-alpha, and the MediaCapabilities API has no alpha flag ([MDN BCD](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/api/MediaCapabilities.json)). Precise codecs strings still matter, because a bare `type="video/mp4"` can leave Chrome showing "a black box with no console error" ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/transparent-video-web-hevc-alpha-ffmpeg)).

The cleaner alternative is Jake Archibald's stacked-alpha technique: one ordinary video with the colour image on top and a greyscale alpha mask below, recombined by a WebGL shader in the `stacked-alpha-video` component ([Jake Archibald](https://jakearchibald.com/2024/video-with-transparency/); [npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/stacked-alpha-video)). It needs no alpha codec, sidesteps the native-alpha playback bugs he found on Android, and was the smallest option in his tests: **460 kB for stacked AV1**, against 1.1 MB for VP9 and 3.4 MB for native HEVC with alpha. The costs are a WebGL dependency (another context alongside any glass), a component last released in January 2025, and the need to re-test his 2024 findings in current browsers. If the hero sits on a flat colour with nothing moving behind the logo, a third option deserves a quick test: a flattened, opaque video, which removes the alpha problem entirely, provided the video's background renders as exactly the page colour in every browser, which is not guaranteed.

Low Power Mode on iOS disables autoplay by design; WebKit closed the bug as "won't fix", and the native play button appears on autoplay videos ([WebKit Bugzilla](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=219889)). MDN confirms that autoplay needs muted video with `playsinline` in Safari, and that `play()` returns a promise which rejects with `NotAllowedError` when blocked ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay)). Josh's WebP fallback is the right idea and needs two additions. Render the still first, as the base layer and poster, then hide the video if `play()` rejects or no `playing` event arrives within about a second and a half, which also hides WebKit's forced play glyph. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, skip the download and show the final frame, the fully formed gold wordmark, which should also hold once the 4.2-second clip ends.

For encoding, start from a ProRes 4444 master. VP9 needs `-pix_fmt yuva420p` and `-auto-alt-ref 0`, because alternate reference frames clash with the alpha stream. HEVC needs macOS's `hevc_videotoolbox` encoder with `-alpha_quality` and the `-tag:v hvc1` flag, without which Safari will not play the file. Strip the audio, and confirm the alpha survived with ffprobe's `alpha_mode` tag ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/transparent-video-web-hevc-alpha-ffmpeg)). Rotato suggests 720p is often enough for short clips ([Rotato](https://rotato.app/read/transparent-videos-for-the-web)), and Jake warns that 8-bit limited-range encodes can band smooth gradients, a real risk for liquid gold ([Jake Archibald](https://jakearchibald.com/2024/video-with-transparency/)). A budget of around 1 to 1.5 MB per format is a sensible working target, not a published standard. Test edge fringing against both cream and black, since semi-transparent edges look different on each. The black sections also flatter gold: as an illustration, a metallic gold such as #D4AF37 has about 10:1 contrast on black but only about 1.6:1 on cream. Logotypes are exempt from WCAG contrast rules, but with his real gold value, staging the hero or a key logo moment in one of the black sections is worth trying.

## Conclusion

The new site is a procurement tool as much as a portfolio. The decisive moments happen out of sight, when a marketing head picks three names to email and a finance officer sets up a new supplier, and two cheap moves serve those moments directly: pricing ladder tiers so their VAT-inclusive totals fall under the quote bands colleges actually use, and publishing company, insurance and accessibility facts on one page. Both speak to the person holding the budget rather than the person browsing, and neither costs anything beyond a few hours of writing. Accessibility is where Josh's pitch and his build meet. A studio selling WCAG-compliant reports and charts to the people who must check them cannot ship a glass navigation that fails contrast or a hero that ignores reduced motion, so his own site's accessibility statement is part of the sales case.

The technical risk sits almost entirely in the two signature effects, and both mostly serve mobile first impressions, while the buyer who sends the enquiry is usually at a desk. Build the plain layer first, fast HTML, legible CSS glass and a still gold wordmark, and treat liquid-glass-js and the alpha video as enhancements that can fail without anyone noticing. Several things remain open and are worth checking before launch: the Agatho licence's terms for web files in a public repo, whose name Pages CMS commits appear under, current Android behaviour for alpha video, Firefox's cross-document transitions, and Web3Forms' data terms. The evidence on availability lines, logo strips and calendar booking is thin, so the best data on those will be his own enquiries in the months after launch.
